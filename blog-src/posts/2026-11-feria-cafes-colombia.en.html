Every good coffee relationship starts by showing up. In October, Mateo and I traveled to Cafés de Colombia 2026, the national specialty coffee expo, to cup, listen and meet the people behind Colombian coffee.

Stop 01 · Arrival

Four days at the fair

We arrived on October 15 and stayed until Sunday, when the fair closed. Every day we talked with coffee growers and with companies running very different processes in other regions of the country.

Stop 02 · Cupping

30 to 40 samples a day

Cupping at that pace gave us a real basis for comparison. We tasted varieties that are not common in our region, different intensities, notes like intense chocolate, raisins, oak and liqueur, and increasingly elaborate processes.

40samples a day, at most

Stop 03 · People

The people we met

We met people from all over the country, but we want to highlight the growers from Antioquia, Caldas and Risaralda. We built a closer relationship with them, because of the quality of their coffee and because we saw a real opportunity to work together. We also want to highlight Huila, which is drawing attention for its excellent co-fermented and infused coffees.

Stop 04 · Origin

Norte de Santander showed up

Norte de Santander was at the fair too, and for us, working with coffee from this region, it was a chance to put it to the test against the rest of the country. Cupping so many daily samples from very different regions let us compare the quality of our region with other areas, including Colombia’s most traditional coffee regions.

The result left us convinced: Norte de Santander has exceptional quality. In that head-to-head comparison its coffees held their own; they were among the best we cupped and, in our opinion, above many regions with a long coffee tradition. We proved it cup by cup, and it confirms that this origin deserves to be shown to the world.
