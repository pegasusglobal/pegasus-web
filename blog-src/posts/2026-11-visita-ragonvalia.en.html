Every coffee we export starts in a specific place, with a specific family. In this post we take you to Ragonvalia, in Norte de Santander, Colombia, where we selected the Castillo lot that is now part of our available coffees.

Why Ragonvalia

Ragonvalia sits in the Eastern Andes, close to the Venezuelan border. Its farms grow coffee among the mountains, at altitudes that give the bean a slow development and a marked sweetness. The lot we selected grows at 1,670 meters above sea level.

How we evaluate a lot

Before we offer a coffee to a café or a roaster, we evaluate it in three steps:

  • Farm visit: we meet the producer and see the process and growing conditions.
  • Cupping: we score the lot's sensory profile using a specialty protocol.
  • Traceability: we record the farm, variety and process behind every bag.

What ends up in the cup

This Castillo from Ragonvalia shows notes of sweet chocolate, caramel, dried fruit, vanilla and a citric finish. It is a balanced coffee that works well both as espresso and in filter methods.

When you know which farm your coffee comes from, you can share that story with your customers.

What this means for your coffee shop

Serving a coffee with a known origin sets you apart: you can show the farm, altitude and process on your menu, and answer with confidence when a customer asks where their coffee comes from.
